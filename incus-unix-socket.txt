● Good question to stop on — let me answer it straight from the code, because
  the answer is no, xray does not use OpenFlow. They're two separate layers that
  gemma configures independently.

  Here's what the code actually shows:

  - gemma generates both — main.rs builds one XrayRoutingMap { xray_routes, 
  openflow_rules } and writes two separate artifacts: the xray config and the
  OpenFlow rules. So gemma feeds both, but they're consumed by different
  components. (That dual output is probably the source of the confusion.)
  - OpenFlow/OVS = L2/L3 packet steering + obfuscation. openflow.rs manages
  flows for the privacy chain gbr_wg → gbr_warp → gbr_xray and the obfuscation
  levels. It steers packets across bridge ports — including into the gbr_xray
  port. It works on match-fields (in_port, dl_type, nw_dst/tp_dst), not domains.
  - xray = L7 SNI/domain routing. xray sits at the gbr_xray port and routes by
  SNI/domain/identity to backends (container ns, or container.sock → tonic-web).
  It has no idea OpenFlow exists.

  So the relationship is a stack, not a dependency:

  WG ingress → [OVS/OpenFlow: gbr_wg → gbr_warp → gbr_xray + obfuscation]  ←
  openflow_rules (gemma)
                                          ↓ (packets handed to the xray port)
                                [xray: SNI/domain routing]                  ←
  xray config.json (gemma)
                                          ↓
                          container ns  /  container.sock → tonic-web

  OpenFlow gets traffic to xray and does the privacy/obfuscation; xray does the
  application-layer routing. Neither calls the other — gemma is the common brain
  that emits config for both.
