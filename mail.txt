  into a root-owned /run/ghostbridge just works, and chattr from inside works
  too. The migration is cheap: 158 MB rootfs, so the uid shift is seconds, and
  every container is currently unprivileged so there's no precedent to break.

  But I'd recommend against it here, mainly because it buys nothing you don't 
  already have. The permission problem is fixed — 0777 restored, the three
  create_dir_all sites patched so a bridge restart can't undo it, and mail is
  serving on all five ports right now. There's no remaining blocker for
  privileged to unblock.

  Against that zero benefit, the cost is the worst one available on this box.
  mail-3tched is your most exposed container: postfix, dovecot and PHP-FPM 
  running SnappyMail, on five public ports. Unprivileged, an RCE in any of those
  gets an attacker a subuid with no NIC and no route — genuinely boxed in.
  Privileged, that same RCE is host root, and from there every other container
  and the blockchain and the sealed blob catalog are reachable. Webmail PHP is
  the single likeliest thing on this host to get popped.

  It also cuts against the identity model directly — the WireGuard/assertion
  path exists so that reaching a service proves who you are. A privileged
  container makes the strongest boundary you have (the userns) decorative for
  the one workload that most needs it.
